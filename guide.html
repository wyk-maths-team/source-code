1. Platform Overview

WYK Maths Team is an online judge built for Olympiad-style and DSE mathematics practice. It is a full submission pipeline: you submit an answer (or a solution process), the system judges it, and results feed into contests, leaderboards, achievements, and your personal progress chart.

  • Platform: Vercel serverless (edge network, global)
  • Data: MongoDB Atlas — submissions, problems, contests, user state
  • Images: Cloudinary (problem figures, wallpapers) and GitHub CDN (PFPs, reports)
  • AI grading: DeepSeek R1 / V3 (via a self-hosted bridge — for the "Steps" mode)
Who is this for? Students preparing for HKMHASC, HKMO, IMO Prelim. Teachers can also create contests and view the marker dashboard.

3. Problem Structure & Types

Every problem has a unique ID and belongs to exactly one of the following structures. The submission UI changes depending on which type you are solving.

3.1 Standard Numeric Problem numeric

  • Answer is a number (integer or decimal).
  • Comparison uses a tolerance of 10−6 — small floating-point differences are accepted.
  • Answer format examples: 29, 3.14159.
  • Answers like 1/2,piare not accepted. The input zone will calculate the value for you.
If the problem suggested answer is not a nuber, the submission may return "System Error". Please use the Report button on the problem page.

3.2 Custom-Logic Problem logic

  • The problem's logic field contains a small judging function.
  • Used for answers that cannot be reduced to a single number — e.g. sets, ordered pairs, symbolic expressions, or answers accepted in multiple equivalent forms.
  • From your perspective, the UI looks identical to a numeric problem: type your answer and submit. The difference is invisible to you.
If the problem author's custom logic contains a bug, the submission may return "System Error". Please use the Report button on the problem page.

3.3 AI-Graded Steps steps

  • Instead of a final answer, you write out your solution steps (up to 1000 characters).
  • An AI model grades your reasoning process and returns a score plus a written comment.
  • See Section 4 for full details.

4. Submission Modes

The answer workbench on every problem page has three tabs. Choose the one that matches how you want to answer.

Text Mode

  • For numeric or short symbolic answers. A live preview strip under the input shows the parsed value — useful for spotting typos in fractions or scientific notation.
  • Answer length is capped at 500 characters.
  • Submission cooldown: 10 submissions per minute per user, plus a 5-second hard cooldown between submissions to prevent accidental double-clicks.

Photo Mode

  • Upload a single image (JPEG / PNG). Keep it under a few MB — huge images slow down the pipeline.
  • Once uploaded, you can replace or remove the image before submitting.
  • Result appears as Pending until a teacher marks it.

Steps Mode AI

Write out your reasoning, not just the answer. The AI will work through your solution line by line.

  1. Type or paste your solution into the textarea (up to 1000 characters).
  2. Pick a model:
    • R1 Slow but accurate — reasons step-by-step before judging. Takes 12–120 seconds. Best for non-trivial problems.
    • V3 Fast — quick judgement, 6–24 seconds. Good for straightforward numeric answers.
  3. Submit. A background worker (the bridge) picks up your task and forwards it to the AI.
  4. Poll for the result — this happens automatically on the page. When done, you see the score and a written comment.
  5. If you recieved something like AI error, please retry, if such issue continues, report to admin.
Why use Steps mode? Even when your final answer is wrong, the AI can credit correct partial reasoning. It will also tell you where things went wrong without revealing the answer.
Anti-injection: The AI is explicitly instructed not to reveal the reference answer, even if you ask directly. Attempts to manipulate the AI will simply be ignored.

5. Judging System

Different problem types use different judges. All results funnel into the same status system.

VerdictMeaning
AcceptedFull credit. The problem's state becomes passed and discussions are unlocked.
Partial Score (N)Only from custom-logic or AI-graded problems. Score between 1 and 99.
Wrong AnswerIncorrect for a numeric, MCQ, or fully-judged problem.
PendingImage submission waiting for manual review.
System ErrorA problem configuration issue (e.g. non-numeric answer key). Report it — it is not your fault unless you wrote units or unintended chars.

After a submission, your problem state updates as follows:

  • passed — once you achieve full credit, this status is permanent. Later wrong attempts cannot undo it.
  • failed — set on first wrong attempt, but can be overwritten by a later pass.
  • not_started — you have not submitted anything yet.

6. Difficulty & Tags

Each problem has a difficulty level shown in the header as Lv.N.

  • Lv.∞ — no difficulty assigned (usually papers or open-ended problems).
  • Lv.1.00 to Lv.7.00+ — an internal rating, higher means harder.
  • You can hide the difficulty indicator in Settings if you prefer to solve without knowing.

Tags

Tags are used by the problem list filter and by the Similar button on each problem. Click Similar to see up to 15 problems sharing the most tags, ranked by tag overlap first and difficulty proximity second.

Bookmarks

Star any problem to add it to your bookmarks. Access the list from the Bookmarks shortcut in the problems page.

7. Contests

Contests are curated sets of problems with a fixed start time and duration. The contest list shows three tabs: All, Live, Upcoming, and Ended.

7.1 Enrolment

  • You can join a contest only before it starts. Once the clock hits the start time, the roster is frozen.
  • You may withdraw yourself before the start; withdrawal is disabled after the start.

7.2 Scoring

  • Numeric problems: full marks on first AC. Each wrong attempt before the first AC applies a 10% penalty to that problem's score: 100 → 90 → 81 → 72.9 → …
  • Partial-score problems (logic): the best score you achieve is kept.
  • Special contests (team events): one wrong attempt permanently locks that problem at 0 points for your team.

7.3 Tie-breaking

  • Primary ranking: total score (descending).
  • Tie-break: the timestamp of your last accepted submission. Earlier submission wins.

7.5 During a Contest

  • The contest page shows your scores and states per problem, updated in real time.
  • Wrong attempts are tracked per problem.
  • Once the contest has ended, full standings and per-problem statistics become available on the results page.

8. Player-vs-Player (PK)

PK is a real-time 1-on-1 competition over a randomised set of questions from one DSE year.

8.1 Flow

  1. Go to another user's profile and click Invite. An invitation link is generated.
  2. The invited user opens the link (/pk/invite?id=…) and clicks Accept. The invitation expires after 10 minutes.
  3. You will only be redirected to the room if you are in the waiting room.
  4. A room is created with a unique ID. Both players land on the room page.
  5. The room contains 9 islands — 6 hexagon islands (Part A, Q1–30) and 3 triangle islands (Part B, Q31–45).
  6. You start with Island 1 unlocked. Correctly solving a problem claims that slot for you.
  7. When you solve a problem on an island, you unlock all adjacent islands.

8.2 Rules

RuleValue
Match duration30 minutes
Problem view time (per problem)4 minutes

Wrong-answer cooldown10 minutes (per problem, per player)

8.3 Spectator Mode

  • Spectators see the full map, both players' scores, and can watch islands being unlocked in real time — but cannot open problems except the starting island

10. Progress & Statistics

  • Home page shows your solved count, total problems, and completion percentage.
  • Profile page (/users/<username>) shows a grid of every problem you have attempted, colour-coded by state.
  • Leaderboard (/ranklist) ranks users by number of passed problems. Online status is shown with a green dot.
  • Progress chart (/charts) plots cumulative solves over time for every active user. Hover for a breakdown by date; click to pin.
  • Submissions (/submissions) lists every submission with filters for user, problem, and result.

11. Important Notes

Privacy & data

  • Your submission content (answer text, uploaded images, solution steps) is stored on the server.
  • Other users cannot see your answer until they have solved the problem, or unless they are an admin.
  • Real names are always shown.

Content quality

  • Some problems were imported from OCR pipelines and may contain formatting glitches or incomplete statements.
  • If a problem looks wrong, use the Report button on the problem page. The report goes to the admin queue with your username attached.
  • Problem IDs may change or disappear over time — do not rely on them as permanent identifiers.

Performance

  • During peak hours, database operations (submissions, favourites, page loads) may be slower.
  • The AI Steps mode depends on a background worker. Very long queues may delay your result by a few minutes.

Anti-cheat & integrity

  • The system logs every action (submission, deletion, admin change) in an audit log.
  • Contest results are public. Any suspicious activity can be reviewed by admins.
  • The AI grader is hardened against prompt injection — you cannot coerce it into revealing the answer.
