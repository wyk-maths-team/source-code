What this platform is

WYK Maths Team is an online judge for MO Exercise.
It is a full submission pipeline: you submit an answer or a solution process, the system judges it, and the result feeds into contests, the leaderboard, achievements and your personal progress chart.

2. Problems & Submission

All the problems are in the Problems tab. Click into a problem to see its statement and submit.

There are 3 submit modes:

Text submit

  • The content in the preview is what you are going to submit.
  • The input zone supports calculation: typing 1/2 gives 0.5 in the preview, sqrt(25) gives 5, sin(pi/6) becomes 0.5. You may refer to MathJS for more symbols.
  • Some problems will specify their own instructions — you may have to input strings like X+1.
  • The judging result of this section is instant. A verdict [Accepted, Wrong Answer, Partial Score, System Error] appears after submission.
  1. Accepted — your answer differs from the model answer by less than 10-6.
  2. Wrong Answer — your answer is incorrect.
  3. Partial Score — only part of your answer is correct. Click the verdict for details.
  4. System Error — either your answer is not a number while the model answer is, or the model answer is not a number while yours is. In this case please report the problem.
Answers are capped at 500 characters. Submissions are rate-limited to 10 per minute per account — this is a guard against accidental double-clicks, not a penalty.

Image submit AI

  • You can upload at most 1 image of your work, and it is sent to the AI for marking.
  • You may replace or remove the image before submitting.
  • You will receive your comments after roughly 10–30 seconds of waiting. The hard server timeout is much longer (about 8 minutes), so a slow queue delays rather than fails.
  • Similar to text, there are 3 possible results. If the image is unreadable the submission stays Pending rather than being scored.

Steps submit AI

  • You write your steps (LaTeX is not necessary) and submit them to the AI.
  • The character limit is 10000.
  • Similar to text, there are 3 possible results.
  • Steps grading costs tokens — the price scales with difficulty. See Section 5.
If you receive an AI error, retry. If the problem persists, report it to an admin — the grader is allowed to fail on malformed input, but repeated failures on the same problem usually mean the problem itself is inconsistent.
Rules for this section
  1. You should not submit pure AI-generated solutions. Only when your solution is too messy for the AI to read clearly may you ask AI to clean it up.

3. Problem Types

Every problem has a unique ID.

3.1 Numeric

  • The reference answer is a number. Comparison uses an absolute tolerance of 10-6.
  • Accepted formats: 29, 3.14159, -0.25. Entering 1/2 or pi is fine because the input zone evaluates it first — the submitted value is the computed number.
  • Most problems fall into this category.

3.2 Custom logic

  • The problem carries a small judging function, used when the answer cannot be reduced to a single number: sets, ordered pairs, symbolic expressions, or several equivalent forms.
  • These problems usually have modified submit zones, or special instructions in statement

3.3 Difficulty

Difficulty is a continuous number shown in the problem header as Lv.N.

  • Lv.1.00 – Lv.9.00+. Roughly: 1–2 is routine, 3–4 is standard competition level, 5+ is hard Olympiad.
  • Lv.∞ means no difficulty has been assigned — usually system test problems.
  • You can hide the indicator in Settings if you prefer to solve without knowing.

3.4 Tags, bookmarks and similar problems

  • Every problem can carry arbitrary tags (Algebra, Geometry, NumberTheory, Combinatorics, plus finer ones). Use the tag filter in the problem list to narrow down.
  • Click Similar on a problem to see up to 15 problems sharing the most tags, ranked by tag overlap first and difficulty proximity second.
  • Star any problem to bookmark it. Your bookmarks are listed under /problems/bookmarked.

4. Judging & Verdicts

VerdictMeaning
AcceptedFull credit. The problem's state becomes passed permanently.
Partial ScoreOnly from custom-logic or AI-graded problems. Any score between 1 and 99; click the verdict for the breakdown.
Wrong AnswerIncorrect. The problem state becomes failed, which a later Accepted overwrites.
PendingAn image submission waiting to be marked.
System ErrorA configuration issue on our side (non-numeric answer key, judge timeout). Note that invalid format of your answer may result in System Error. If the answer is obviously a number, report the problem. Thank you.

Problem state

  • passed — once you earn full credit this is permanent. Later wrong attempts cannot undo it.
    • Note: If your submission was flagged by an admin for abuse, your solve stats on this problem will be cleared. The submission itself will also be marked Wrong Answer. You will receive a System Notice after being flagged.
  • failed — set on the first wrong attempt, overwritten by a later pass.
  • not_started — you have never submitted.
Why a verdict and not just "right/wrong"? Because the three submit modes have genuinely different failure modes.
A numeric judge can only be right or wrong; an AI grader can credit partial reasoning and provide ways to solve.
Collapsing all of these into "Wrong Answer" would make the result page useless for learning.

5. Tokens

Tokens are the platform currency. They gate the special features (AI grading, themes) and they are what PK matches are played for.

5.1 Two balances

BucketStarts atBehaviour
daily30,000Refills to 30,000 every day (Hong Kong time). Spending draws from here first. Unspent daily does not accumulate.
global100,000Permanent. Only touched when daily is exhausted. Rewards and prize money are paid into this bucket.

5.2 Earning

  • Daily login — 10,000 tokens, once per day.
  • Daily quest — 30,000 tokens for solving at least one problem at or above your target difficulty, once per day. Your target difficulty is derived from your rating.
  • Achievements — a one-off reward when unlocked, ranging from 5,000 (common) to 250,000 (legendary). See Section 8.
  • PK matches — the winner's share of the prize pool.

5.3 Spending

  • Steps grading — charged at difficulty × 3,000. A Lv.4 problem costs 12,000; a Lv.8 problem costs 24,000.
    Note: the result cost varies, this number is the least number of tokens you need to submit to that problem.
  • Themes — 300,000 tokens for each change.
  • PK stake — minimum 10,000 per match. See Section 7.
Daily tokens will be used before consuming Global tokens.

6. Contests

6.1 Enrolment

  • You can join anytime during the contest.
  • You may withdraw yourself before the start; withdrawal is disabled afterwards.

6.2 Scoring

  • Numeric problems: full marks on first Accepted. Each wrong attempt before that applies a 10% penalty: 100 → 90 → 81 → 72.9 → …
  • Partial-score problems: your best score is kept.

6.3 Tie-breaking

  • Primary: total score, descending.
  • Secondary: the timestamp of your last accepted submission — earlier wins.

6.4 During and after

  • The contest page shows your score and state per problem, updated live.
  • Wrong attempts are tracked per problem and drive the penalty above.
  • Once the contest ends, full standings and per-problem statistics appear on the results page.

7. Player-vs-Player (PK)

A PK match is a head-to-head race over 3 problems in 30 minutes, with both players staking tokens. It is not about solving everything — it is about solving early.

7.1 Sending a challenge

  1. Open another user's profile and press Invite.
  2. Enter a stake. The minimum is 10,000, and the amount is deducted immediately and held in the pool.
  3. If they decline, cancel, or the invitation expires after 10 minutes, the stake is refunded exactly as it was taken.
  4. You can watch and cancel your outgoing challenges at /pk-bet-challenges.

7.2 Accepting

  • An incoming challenge appears as a notification in the corner of any page you are on, showing the stake.
  • Accepting deducts the same stake from you. You need to cover it in full.
  • Both players must be able to pay; otherwise the match cannot start.

7.3 The match

RuleValue
Duration30 minutes
Problems3, public, chosen near the difficulty implied by your average rating
Excluded problemsAny problem either player has already solved
Fee20% of the pool is taken before settlement
Each problem's score(1800 − T) × S, where T is the second you last submitted (1–1800) and S is your score on it
Which attempt countsYour last submission per problem — a later wrong answer replaces an earlier correct one
WinnerHigher total across the three problems
Answer on the real problem page. The room shows only colour blocks, no statements. Click a block to open /problems/<id> in a new tab and answer there as usual; every submission you make during the window is counted automatically.

Block colours:

  • Red — attempted, scored 0. You may retry.
  • Amber — partial credit.
  • Green — full credit on that problem.
  • Grey — not attempted yet.

7.4 Finishing early

  • Either player may press Announce finish. The other player sees that you are ready.
  • When both have pressed it, the match ends immediately and settles on the submissions made so far.
  • Pressing again withdraws your readiness.

7.5 Settlement

The pool is paid out by score share, with a subsidy for the player who is behind in tokens:

  • The weaker side (lower total score) receives prize × share × F.
  • The stronger side receives the remainder, always non-negative.
  • F is between 1 and 1.25, rising as the token gap between the two players widens. With equal balances F = 1 and the split is a plain score share.

Every match produces a permanent result page at /pk-bet-result?id=… showing both players' per-problem attempts, times, weights and payouts. It does not expire.

Rewards by rarity

common 5,000 uncommon 15,000 rare 40,000 epic 100,000 legendary 250,000

Rewards go into your global balance, not daily. Unlocking everything is worth about 2,000,000 tokens.

You can see how close you are. Locked achievements show a progress bar (63 / 100). The accuracy group shows gate progress instead (8/20 tried) until you have attempted enough problems for the ratio to be meaningful.
Counting is by distinct problem, not by submission. Submitting the same problem fifty times counts as one problem.

9. Progress & Statistics

Rating

Your rating is derived from the difficulties of the problems you have solved. It is not a simple average — harder problems weigh more, the very hardest are softly capped so one lucky solve cannot inflate you, and the whole figure is scaled by a confidence factor that rises with your solved count. A brand-new account sits at 1000.

The rating determines your tier (the colour around your name), your target difficulty for the daily quest, and the difficulty band from which PK problems are drawn.

Where to look

  • Home — solved count, total problems, completion percentage.
  • Profile (/users/<username>) — a grid of every problem you have attempted, colour-coded by state, plus your achievements.
  • Leaderboard (/ranklist) — ranked by problems passed, with an online dot. Supports all-time and rolling daily / weekly / monthly views.
  • Charts (/charts) — cumulative solves over time for every active user. Hover for a per-date breakdown.
  • Submissions (/submissions) — every submission with filters by user, problem and result.

12. Important Notes

Privacy & data

  • Your submission content — answer text, uploaded images, solution steps — is stored on the server.
  • Other users cannot see your answers until they have solved the problem themselves, unless they are an admin.
  • Real names are always shown.

Content quality

  • Many problems were imported from OCR pipelines and may contain formatting glitches or truncated statements. Problems whose judging chain is known to be broken are excluded from PK draws.
  • If a problem looks wrong, use the Report button on the problem page. The report reaches the admin queue with your username attached.
  • Problem IDs can change or disappear. Do not rely on them as permanent identifiers.

Performance

  • During peak hours, database operations may be slower.
  • The AI modes depend on external model providers. A long queue delays your result rather than losing it.

Integrity

  • Every action — submission, deletion, admin change — is written to an audit log.
  • Contest results are public and can be reviewed by admins afterwards.
  • The AI grader is hardened against prompt injection: asking it to reveal the reference answer does not work.
  • Token balances are audited. Attempting to exploit a balance edge case will be reversed and may cost you access.
